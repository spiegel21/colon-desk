Exchange rate

MONEX dollar–colón market

Each candle is one trading session: its open, high, low and close price in colones per dollar. The orange line is the session's VWAP (volume-weighted average price, BCCR indicator 3439). Bars below show dollars traded per session. Drag the handles under the chart to pick a custom window.

BCCR foreign-exchange intervention, cumulative

Dollars the central bank bought minus dollars it sold in MONEX, day by day, in US$ millions (BCCR table 850). Above zero the BCCR has been a net buyer of dollars, which adds colones and leans against a stronger colón. The solid line adds up stabilization operations only, the BCCR's intervention to calm the exchange rate (including its interventions at the old exchange-rate band limits, 2006–2015). The dashed line also adds the BCCR's own-account purchases for its reserves and its MONEX purchases to cover dollars it sold to the SPNB. Bars show each day's net amount by type. Totals restart from the beginning of the range selected at the top.

MONEX vs the official reference rate

MONEX VWAP minus the midpoint of the BCCR reference buy and sell rates, in colones. Above zero, the market paid more for dollars than the reference rate implied.

Exchange-rate volatility

How much the dollar price moves day to day: the standard deviation of daily VWAP changes over the last 20 sessions, scaled to a yearly figure.

Prices and activity

Inflation vs target

Year-on-year change in the consumer price index (CPI) and the median 12-month inflation forecast from the BCCR's expectations survey. The green band is the central bank's target: 3% plus or minus 1 percentage point.

Economic activity (IMAE)

The monthly index of economic activity, a monthly stand-in for GDP. Bars show its year-on-year growth, using the smoothed trend-cycle series (2022 = 100).

Foreign direct investment (FDI) into Costa Rica, by regime

Quarterly inflows in US$ millions, split by the legal regime the receiving company operates under. Free trade zones (zonas francas) are tax-advantaged parks, mostly medical devices and services exporters. Negative values mean foreign owners took money out (disinvestment or loan repayments). Shows at least 5 years.

Glossary

BCCR
Banco Central de Costa Rica, the central bank and the source of every number on this page.
SDDE
Sistema de Divulgación de Datos Económicos, the BCCR's economic data service and API this page reads from.
CRC / ₡
Costa Rican colón. USD / $: US dollar. EUR: euro.
USD/CRC
Colones per US dollar. A higher number means a weaker colón.
MONEX
Mercado de Monedas Extranjeras, the BCCR-run wholesale market where banks and brokers trade dollars for colones each business day.
VWAP
Volume-weighted average price: the average price of all trades in a session, weighted by the dollars traded. The BCCR calls it promedio ponderado.
Open / high / low / close
First, highest, lowest and last price traded in a MONEX session; together they draw each candle.
Reference rate
The BCCR's official daily buy (compra) and sell (venta) exchange rates, computed from the previous day's market trades.
FX intervention
Intervención cambiaria: the BCCR buying or selling dollars in MONEX. Net purchases add colones to the economy and support the dollar price; net sales do the opposite.
Stabilization operations
Operaciones de estabilización: discretionary BCCR trades meant to smooth sharp exchange-rate moves. Before February 2015 the colón moved inside a band, and the BCCR also had to buy at its floor (límite inferior) and sell at its ceiling (límite superior); this page counts those as stabilization too.
Own-account operations
Operaciones propias: dollars the BCCR buys in MONEX for its own needs, mainly to build reserves.
SPNB
Sector público no bancario, the non-bank public sector (state companies such as RECOPE and ICE, and the central government). The BCCR sells them dollars directly, off the market, and buys dollars in MONEX to replenish what it sold; only those MONEX purchases count toward the dashed line.
Volatility
Standard deviation of daily price changes, annualized by √252 (trading days per year). Higher means bigger day-to-day swings.
TPM
Tasa de política monetaria, the central bank's policy rate. It anchors short-term colón interest rates.
TBP
Tasa básica pasiva, the benchmark colón deposit rate, a weighted average of what financial institutions pay on deposits. Many loans in colones are priced off it.
TRI
Tasa de referencia interbancaria, the interbank reference rate the BCCR publishes for each term (1 week to 60 months), in colones and in dollars.
SOFR
Secured Overnight Financing Rate, the main US dollar overnight benchmark.
Treasury bill / note / bond
US government debt: bills mature within a year, notes in 2–10 years, bonds in 20–30 years.
Yield curve
Interest rates plotted against the length of the loan. An upward slope means longer loans pay more.
CPI (IPC)
Consumer price index (Índice de precios al consumidor). Its year-on-year change is the headline inflation rate.
Inflation target
The BCCR aims for 3% inflation with a tolerance of ±1 percentage point.
IMAE
Índice mensual de actividad económica, the monthly index of economic activity, used as a monthly proxy for GDP. The trend-cycle version removes seasonal and irregular swings.
GDP
Gross domestic product, the value of everything produced in the economy.
RIN / NIR
Reservas internacionales netas, the central bank's net international reserves.
FDI (IED)
Foreign direct investment (inversión extranjera directa): money a foreign owner with a lasting stake of 10% or more puts into a Costa Rican company, including new equity, reinvested earnings and loans from the parent company.
Free trade zone
Zona franca: a tax-incentive regime for exporters, home to most medical-device and business-services investment.
Inward processing
Perfeccionamiento activo: a customs regime that lets companies import inputs duty-free to process and re-export them.
Regular companies
Empresas regulares: companies under the ordinary tax regime, outside any special regime.
GAM
Gran Área Metropolitana, the Greater Metropolitan Area around San José, Heredia, Alajuela and Cartago.
ISIC letters
Sector codes from the UN's International Standard Industrial Classification, for example C = manufacturing, K = finance and insurance.
pp
Percentage points: the plain difference between two percentages (3% to 4% is +1 pp).
bps
Basis points: hundredths of a percent (25 bps = 0.25%).
y/y
Year-on-year: compared with the same period a year earlier.
4Q
Sum of the last four quarters, a rolling one-year total.
